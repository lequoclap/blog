## 8) Targeted Recovery Strategies (Adaptive Recovery Protocol)
- Sleep architecture (deep sleep focus)
  - 60–90 min pre-bed wind-down: low light, screens off, light mobility (thoracic/shoulder opening for swim), 5–8 min nasal downshift breathing (e.g., 6 breaths/min or 4–7–8).
  - Thermal: cool room (17–19°C), warm extremities; short warm shower before bed can enhance sleep onset.
  - Nutrition: protein-forward dinner (30–40 g), plus a small low-fiber carbohydrate snack 60–90 min pre-bed if dinner was early; avoid heavy fats late.
  - Supplements (if tolerated): magnesium glycinate 200–300 mg 60–90 min pre-bed.
- Autonomic tone and HRV
  - Two mini-sessions/day of coherence breathing (5 min each, morning and late afternoon).
  - 10–15 min easy post-dinner walk to aid parasympathetic shift and glycemic control.
  - Morning bright light exposure (5–10 min) to anchor circadian timing.
- Hydration (assume moderate sweat rates)
  - Baseline: 30–35 ml/kg/day (~2.1–2.5 L/day at 70 kg), plus training losses.
  - Pre: 5 ml/kg with 300–600 mg sodium 60–90 min before sessions.
  - During: 0.4–0.6 L/h with 500–700 mg sodium per liter for sessions ≥60 min (adjust if cool).
  - Post: ~1.2–1.5 L per kg body mass lost (or 500–750 ml per training hour if no scale).
- Training timing (given new swim skill)
  - Schedule technique-heavy swims earlier in the day where possible; avoid late-evening sessions that raise nighttime sympathetic tone.
  - Avoid stacking high-cognitive-load swim drills on the same day as threshold run/bike until sleep rebounds.
