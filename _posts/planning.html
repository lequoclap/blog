4) Microcycle & Load Management

  • Loading rhythm: 3 weeks build + 1 week deload in Phases 3–5; Phase 1 descends (taper); Phase 2 is recovery.
  • Deload week: Volume −25–35%, drop intensity density, keep frequency.
  • Key‑session density: Typically 2–3 “work” sessions/week total; never stack all quality on adjacent days.
  • Recovery anchors: ≥1 low‑load day weekly; mobility most days; prioritize sleep and fueling post long/brick sessions.

5) Phase Transition Gates (Summary)

  • Into Phase 2: B race complete; soreness resolves within 3–5 days; no new injuries.
  • Into Phase 3: Easy back‑to‑back aerobic days tolerated; foundational strength patterns pain‑free.
  • Into Phase 4: Long ride 2–3 h; long run 75–90 min without form breakdown; swim stroke stable in late reps.
  • Into Phase 5: Weekly brick tolerated; fueling/hydration tested; tempo/sweet spot consistent.
  • Into Taper (starts 2026‑04‑26): Long brick clean; no escalating aches; readiness steady.

6) Notes for Downstream Planners

  • Pacing language: “HIM pace” = sustainably hard, sub‑threshold steady state appropriate for ~2.5–3 h bike and ~90 min run; translate to athlete‑specific zones.
  • Heat strategy: Begin acclimation in Phase 5; include one weekly session mimicking Danang timing if available.
  • Nutrition: Progressive carbohydrate practice on long rides/runs from Phase 4; finalize race nutrition in Phase 5.
  • Equipment: Phase 5 is rehearsal window for race wheels/tires, wetsuit or swimskin, and transition flow. No major changes after taper begins.
  • Contingency: If post‑B recovery lingers, extend Phase 2 by 1 week and compress Phase 3 by 1 week (protect load integrity vs forcing intensity).

Execution notes and guardrails

  • Intensity distribution: Keep the whole 14 days ~75/20/5 (Z1–2/Z3/Z4–5). Strides/hill sprints are short neuromuscular touches and do not add high metabolic load.
  • Spike control: Avoid single‑day jumps >20% vs typical; keep ACWR ≤1.2 during this build.
  • Swim progress: Favor 100–200 m repeats with short rest; build continuous capacity. Technique first.
  • Bike progress: One quality (SS or torque) per week is sufficient in this phase; the rest aerobic.
  • Run emphasis: Two quality exposures total (long run HM segments + cruise intervals). Everything else easy with brief strides.

What “good” feels like by Nov 21

  • Long run done at steady HR with tidy form; HM‑pace segments feel measured, not frantic.
  • Bike SS feels controlled at RPE 6–7 with stable HR.
  • Swims consolidate into repeatable 100–200 m sets without form breakdown.
  • Sleep and RHR trend slightly better than start; no accumulating niggles.
If readiness remains amber for 2+ consecutive days, trim volume to the low end and remove the final rep in quality sessions. If red flags persist, swap quality for aerobic technique and notify the team.
